The California-Grape Difference

One winery in all of Door County made this call in 1993. And held to it.

Every other winery on the peninsula built its identity around Wisconsin fruit: cherry, cranberry, apple, cold-hardy hybrids. Russell Turco went the other direction.

"What if we visited small California vineyards, found great grapes, shipped them to Door County, and made wine?" That question became Stone's Throw.

The grapes arrive refrigerated, 2,500 miles from harvest, and are crushed within 36 hours. No shortcuts. Each lot goes into its own small tank, ferments, and ages in a single barrel before it ever becomes a bottle. That is what "one barrel at a time" actually means in practice.

The result is a tasting room unlike any other on the Door County wine trail: cabernet, zinfandel, syrah, chardonnay, pinot noir from named California AVAs, poured where you can hear the lake.

Current Releases

Small-batch wines from three California regions

Each wine at Stone's Throw traces back to a specific vineyard in Napa, Sonoma, or Mendocino. No commodity fruit. No large-tank blending. Every bottle is a product of micro-vinification from a single barrel.

A 28-year wager on California fruit, placed in the middle of Door County cherry country

"With high quality grapes, attention to detail, and small tank fermentation, fine wine is created."

Russell Turco, Founder and Winemaker

Russell started with a question in 1993: what if the best winery you could build in Door County sourced its fruit from Napa, Sonoma, and Mendocino instead of growing it here?

Three years of research. Relationships built with small California growers willing to part with premium fruit. The logistics of refrigerated transport across 2,500 miles, timed so the grapes arrive and are crushed within 36 hours of harvest. That is the infrastructure that makes Stone's Throw possible.

The first vintage, a 1996 Zinfandel, proved the model worked. The winery opened in 1997, and the program has run continuously on the same three California regions ever since. The winemaker has not changed. The philosophy has not changed. The fruit sources have deepened.

National wine press noticed. Wine Spectator, Wine Enthusiast, and Food Network have each covered the story. Door County Magazine named Stone's Throw Best Winery. The Lux Life award for Best Wisconsin Wine Bar went to Vino Vino, the sister venue on the same property.

Sister Venue

Vino Vino at STW
Tuscan wine and tapas on the property

Vino Vino is the wine bar and tapas kitchen that shares the Baileys Harbor property with the winery. Styled after the stuzzichini bars Russell visited in Tuscany, it is the place where a glass of the current releases meets a plate built from local Wisconsin ingredients and garden-grown vegetables.

Lux Life named Vino Vino the Best Wisconsin Wine Bar. It is the only place in Door County where you can pair Napa-sourced Cabernet with Neuske's and BelGioioso cheese and call it an afternoon.
